# EntheoGen Research Mode

EntheoGen Research Mode is a public beta interaction guide for educational
harm-reduction research. It helps readers compare entheogenic plants,
psychedelics, medicines, and adjacent substance classes using a curated
evidence dataset.

This project is not medical, psychiatric, legal, or therapeutic advice. It does
not diagnose, prescribe, clear anyone for use, or replace a qualified clinician.
If someone may be experiencing toxicity, serotonin syndrome, hypertensive
crisis, seizure, collapse, severe agitation, psychosis, or another emergency,
seek urgent medical help.

## Beta Status

The app is a private student project beta test version. Labels, explanations,
and evidence links are actively reviewed as the dataset improves. Treat every
readout as a research summary with uncertainty, not as a personal safety
clearance.

## What The Guide Shows

The interaction guide presents a risk label, confidence signal, mechanism family,
field notes, and rule-based context for selected pairs. A high-risk label means
the combination should be avoided outside appropriate clinical oversight. A
lower-risk or unknown label does not mean a combination is safe for a specific
person.

The guide is intentionally conservative when evidence is limited, when severe
outcomes are plausible, or when overlapping mechanisms create practical risk.

## Data Provenance

The dataset is assembled from curated source notes, reviewed claims,
pharmacology references, systematic reviews, clinical education material,
traditional-context notes, and explicit mechanistic inference. Source material is
tracked in the repository knowledge base and linked into the app-facing dataset
where possible.

Dataset records may include direct human evidence, observational reports,
mechanistic evidence, field consensus, indirect evidence, or explicit source
gaps. Confidence labels are intended to make those differences visible.

## Uncertainty

Many entheogen and medication combinations do not have controlled
co-administration studies. Some readouts therefore depend on indirect evidence,
case reports, known pharmacology, clinical caution, or extrapolation from related
substance classes.

Use the uncertainty signals. Low confidence, inferred mechanisms, source gaps,
and theoretical labels mean the readout needs extra caution and should not be
treated as settled evidence.

## References

The app includes a current reference list in the menu. Key sources currently
include:

- Alma Healing Centre, Ayahuasca and medications interactions database.
- Gillman, P. K. (2005), monoamine oxidase inhibitors, opioid analgesics, and
  serotonin toxicity.
- Halman et al. (2024), drug-drug interactions involving classic psychedelics.
- Malcolm (2023), ayahuasca and drug interaction teaching material.
- Ruffell et al. (2023), review of ayahuasca history, pharmacology, and
  therapeutic aspects.

The source corpus and current app reference list live in the canonical
repository.

## Harm-Reduction Framing

Avoid combining substances when a readout indicates dangerous or poorly
characterized risk. Avoid stacking multiple serotonergic, stimulant,
cardiovascular, sedative, lithium, antipsychotic, MAOI, or seizure-threshold
burdens without qualified medical review.

Set and setting do not remove pharmacological risk. Screening, dose reduction,
spacing, sober support, and emergency planning can reduce some risks, but they
do not make high-risk combinations safe.
